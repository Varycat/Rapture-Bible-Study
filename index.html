Rapture of the Church

A Bible-first study comparing the pre-tribulation rapture interpretation with the Seventh-day Adventist interpretation. Tap any Bible reference to read the passage.
1

Does the Bible teach a rapture?

Biblical observation

Paul explicitly describes believers being caught up to meet Christ. The dead in Christ rise first, and living believers are gathered with them.

Seventh-day Adventist interpretation

Adventists affirm this event. The disagreement is whether it is a separate secret event before the tribulation or part of Christ's visible Second Coming.

Conclusion: The rapture is explicit. Its timing must be determined from the surrounding passages.
2

Is the rapture described as secret?

Biblical observation

Paul describes a shout, the voice of the archangel and the trumpet of God. Revelation says every eye will see Christ.

Seventh-day Adventist interpretation

The Adventist reading understands this as a literal, audible, visible and glorious Second Coming.

Conclusion: These passages do not explicitly describe the event as secret.
3

The crucial sequence in Matthew 24

Biblical observation

Jesus places the visible coming of the Son of Man and gathering of His elect after the tribulation, accompanied by a great trumpet.

Seventh-day Adventist interpretation

Adventists understand this gathering as part of the visible Second Coming following the final crisis.

Conclusion: Tribulation → Christ appears → Trumpet → Elect gathered.
4

Our gathering to Christ and the man of sin

Biblical observation

Paul introduces Christ's coming and our gathering unto Him, then discusses apostasy and the lawless one.

Seventh-day Adventist interpretation

The passage does not explicitly say the church disappears before the man of sin appears.

Conclusion: A pre-tribulation removal must be inferred; it is not directly stated here.
5

The resurrection and the last trumpet

Biblical observation

Paul connects transformation and resurrection with the last trumpet.

Seventh-day Adventist interpretation

Adventists connect this transformation with the resurrection at Christ's Second Coming and 1 Thessalonians 4.

Conclusion: Resurrection, transformation, trumpet and gathering naturally fit together.
6

"Not appointed to wrath"

Biblical observation

Pre-tribulation interpreters often use verse 9 to argue believers are removed before final judgments.

Seventh-day Adventist interpretation

Adventists distinguish divine wrath from tribulation or persecution; preservation does not necessarily require prior removal.

Conclusion: The text promises salvation from divine wrath but does not by itself date the rapture.
7

"I will come again and receive you"

Biblical observation

Jesus promises to return and receive His followers.

Seventh-day Adventist interpretation

Both interpretations affirm the promise. The passage itself does not specify a seven-year interval or two separate future comings.

Conclusion: Other passages are needed to determine its timing.

Overall Bible-first conclusion

The clearest passages support a real catching up of believers. Read together, they present a visible and glorious return of Christ associated with the trumpet, resurrection, transformation and gathering of believers.

Assessment: On this specific question, that sequence is closer to the Seventh-day Adventist understanding of the rapture as part of the Second Coming than to a secret pre-tribulation rapture followed years later by another visible coming.
